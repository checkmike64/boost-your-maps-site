Pest control SEO: 12 fixes to get found on Google Maps

Pest control SEO means getting your company in front of people nearby who search Google for pest control. That's the map and the results under it. Your Google Business Profile, the page it links to, your reviews and links from other sites decide how often you show up. So does how close you are to the person searching.

How does Google choose the pest control companies it shows first?

Google says it looks at how well a profile fits the search, how far the business is from the person searching, and how well known it is. You can't move your office closer to every customer. Most of the rest of the profile is yours to change, and reviews and links help people know your name.

For most pest control companies, SEO means local SEO. In our study data, 280 of the 294 listings had the same main category, “Pest control service.” So the category alone won't make you stand out. Your list of services, your photos, your posts and your site tell people which pests you treat.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, “Tips to improve your local ranking on Google.”

Pest control SEO, step by step

We work through these 12 fixes on every pest control profile, in this order.

1. Which categories should a pest control company pick?

Your main category should be “Pest control service.” Add more only for work you do, like animal control, bird control or bee removal. Each one tells Google you fit those searches too.

2. How should you list your services?

Add each service the way a homeowner would type it: termite inspection, mosquito control, rodent control, bed bug treatment. Give each one a short, plain description. Start with the pests your city searches for most. In our study of pest searches, mosquito searches came out ahead in 7 of the 15 cities. In 9 cities Keyword Planner lumps rodent searches in with “pest control,” so rodent work may be bigger there than it looks. Google Ads Keyword Planner is free with a Google Ads account and shows rough figures for your own city.

3. What name should go on your profile?

Google's rules say the profile name should match the name on your sign, trucks and paperwork. Adding a service or a city that isn't part of that name breaks the rules. Google suspends profiles with keyword names when it catches them or a competitor reports them, and getting one back can take weeks.

4. Should you show your address or a service area?

Show the address only if customers can visit you there. If your techs go to homes and nobody comes to the office, Google asks you to hide the address and list the areas you serve. Don't list a virtual office or a mailbox to look closer to a city. That can get the profile suspended.

5. Where should the website button go?

The website button should open a page about the area that profile serves. Put your phone number near the top of that page. Add a tag to the end of the link, like ?utm_source=gbp, so your website stats can count visits from the profile. A homeowner who taps it should land on your number, the towns you cover and the pests you treat.

6. How do you get more reviews the right way?

Have your techs ask every customer for a review after each job, with a short link they can text. Google's rules ban asking only the happy ones, and they ban paying for reviews. Answer every review, good or bad, within a few days. Most established companies already do this. At the 23 whose newest reviews we read, owners had answered 309 of 460.

7. How often should you add photos and posts?

Add photos every month of your trucks, your team and jobs you've done, like a termite treatment or a sealed crack under a door. Add posts through the month about the pests that are out right now. Photos of your own trucks and techs show homeowners who will come to the door.

8. Why do your name, address and phone need to match everywhere?

Directories and map sites, which SEO companies call citations, copy your details. Wrong details on those sites send customers to an old phone line and make your profile look less reliable. Check the big ones first: Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp, the BBB, Angi, Nextdoor and your state pest control association. Fix any old phone number or address.

9. What pages does your website need?

Your website needs a page for each main service, like termites, mosquitoes and rodents. Add a page for each area you serve where you have something to say about it. Put the service and the place in the page title and the main heading. Make the pages load fast on a phone and show your phone number near the top. Add structured data (schema) that matches your profile. Google's spam rules ban pages that only swap the city name, so give each page its own photos, jobs and answers.

10. How do you earn links from other websites?

A link from another site tells Google that someone vouches for you. Good ones for a pest control company come from your state pest control association, your suppliers, the chamber of commerce and local news. A team or event you sponsor may list you too. Google wants paid links like that marked as sponsored, so they help your name in town more than your ranking. Don't buy links from sites that sell them, because Google's spam rules treat that as a link scheme.

11. When should you start the work?

Profile work usually takes 60 to 90 days to show up on the map, so start about three months before your busiest weeks. In 14 of the 15 cities we studied, the slowest month for “pest control” plus the city name came between November and February. That makes winter a good time to fix the profile and the pages.

12. What should you track each month?

Your profile's Performance tab counts calls, website clicks and direction requests each month. Your website stats, with the tag from step 5, show what those visitors do next. Keep your own business number as the main number on the profile, since Google's rules say that number must be under your direct control. Each month, look at the same three counts next to how many people searched for pest control near you.

Sources for steps 3, 4, 6, 9, 10 and 12: Google, “Guidelines for representing your business on Google,” Google Search's spam policies and the Google Maps user-contributed content policy.

Does AI search change pest control SEO?

Google now shows an AI answer at the top of many searches, including “pest control seo” itself. Some homeowners also ask ChatGPT or Gemini who to call. AI answers lean on the places a person would check. Those are your Google profile, your reviews, your website, directories, and lists of the best companies in town. When they agree on who you are, what you treat and where you work, you're more likely to show up in the answer.

Step 10 matters most here. A company that other trusted sites mention by name has a better chance of being named in an AI answer.

How to pick a pest control SEO company

Ask any company you talk to these six questions before you sign.

  • Do you promise a spot? Rankings are Google's call. Ask what the company will do for you each month instead.
  • Will you change our business name? Adding keywords or a city to your name breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended.
  • Where do reviews come from? They should come from your customers, asked after every job. Bought, written or filtered reviews put the whole profile at risk.
  • Who owns the profile and the website? Ask what you keep if you leave.
  • What will the monthly report show? Ask to see a sample, and check that you can follow it.
  • How long is the contract? Ask what it costs to leave and how much notice you need to give.
