Swarm season decides when you should start

For a Tampa pest control company, the busy months are easy to predict. Asian subterranean termites start swarming at dusk in March, Formosan termites follow in late April, and drywood termites keep going into June. In 2023, University of Florida scientists confirmed that Asian subterranean termites had settled in South Tampa, so if your trucks cover South Tampa, plan for swarm calls there each spring. After that, the rainy season runs from late May to early October. Standing water lets mosquitoes breed, and they surge again in the weeks after a hurricane.

Since profile work usually takes 60 to 90 days to show up on the map, December or early January is the time to begin if you want to be near the top when swarm calls start. We plan your posts around the same calendar, so a post about swarmers goes up the week homeowners start finding wings.

Why a Brandon office struggles to show up in Westchase

Google ranks map results by how relevant a business is, how far it is from the person searching, and how well known it is. Your office location is the one part you can't change. A company based in Brandon will have a hard time showing up for someone searching in Westchase, on the far side of town, and it gets harder still across the bay in Pinellas County.

So we build your profile around the neighborhoods closest to your office first. Your monthly report shows how far your reach goes, and we'll tell you which parts of town are hard to reach.

Your next customers are searching from Riverview and Brandon

More than two-thirds of Hillsborough County lives outside Tampa's city limits, in places like Brandon, Riverview, Carrollwood and Westchase. Riverview alone grew from about 71,000 people in 2010 to more than 107,000 in 2020. Homeowners search using those place names, so your service list, business description and posts should use them too. A profile that only says "Tampa" leaves those searches to the companies that name the suburb.

What we handle

What we'd fix first on a Tampa pest control profile

  • Your main category. Google gives this trade one choice, "Pest control service," so the rest of the profile has to spell out termites, mosquitoes and everything else you treat.

  • Your JB license number. Every Florida pest control license starts with JB, and homeowners can look yours up on the state website. We put it and your certified categories in your business description.

  • Your service list. Termite inspection, wood-destroying organism (WDO) inspection for home sales, mosquito control and the rest of what you offer, up to 50 services, each with a short description Google can read.

  • Your service area. Most pest control customers never visit the office, so Google asks you to hide the address and set a service area. We set it to match where your trucks go.

  • Your reviews. Your reviews stay yours, and we never write or buy them. We keep the rest of the profile as complete as possible and give you a simple plan for asking customers after a job goes well.
